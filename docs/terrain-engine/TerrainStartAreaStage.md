# TerrainStartAreaStage

Generation stage: the last step in the terrain-generation pipeline, run by `TerrainFieldBuilder` as `"Start areas"` straight after `TerrainStartPositionStage` (FEAT-09; the start distance and the neutral sites are FEAT-14).

`TerrainStartPositionStage` chooses start tiles; a playable start is an area. This stage reserves an area around every chosen start, validates it, places the scenario's start kit in it, and writes one `TerrainStartAreaReport` per start. It follows how the genre does it: Ensemble's random-map patent gives each player one contiguous area with a minimum distance between areas and water excluded, then places a per-player object kit in a nested loop per area and per object type, relaxing a critical object's constraints in a fixed order; Age of Empires II grows every player land from its origin at once until they meet; 0 A.D. gives every base the same kit at authored distances. It never re-scores or moves a start. Unusable starts stay where they are and are reported with the reason.

Where asked, it also measures how far every tile is from the nearest start and makes the far country's underground richer by it. That is Factorio's rule: resources are richer the farther they are from spawn. It also places the kit's Neutral entries between the starts, as Age of Empires puts neutral objects between player lands.

## Public API

- `static void Apply(TerrainGenerationBuffer world, TerrainGenerationSettings settings, TerrainStartKitRules kit, TerrainResourceRules resources, CancellationToken cancellation = default)` — the sole entry point. Returns immediately when there are no start positions. With `settings.StartDistanceScaling` and `settings.StartAreaRadius` both 0 it allocates nothing, so a map using neither is built exactly as before.

Everything else (`MeasureStartDistance`, `ScaleUndergroundRichness`, `PlaceNeutralSites`, `NeutralBand`, `Claimable`, `TooCloseToOtherArea`, `CountExits`, `Definition`, `PlaceEntry`, `Place`, `Shortfall`, `Overwritable`, `SpacedFromSame`, `StampDeposit` and the constants `KitHashSalt`, `KitSpacing` = 4, `UndergroundDiscRadius` = 2, `NeutralBandCells` = 2) is private to the `internal static class TerrainStartAreaStage`.

### What Apply does

1. **Start distance and richness, when `StartDistanceScaling` is above 0 (FEAT-14).** This step runs first and does not depend on `StartAreaRadius`: distance from the starts is a fact about the starts, not about their areas.
   - **Distance.** A mask of the in-bounds start cells goes through `TerrainEuclideanDistance.Squared` (the float overload, written into `FloatScratchA`). That is the one exact Euclidean transform the shoreline stage and the coast field already use. The square root, rounded to the nearest whole cell and clamped to `ushort`, is written to `world.CellStartDistance`. Water is measured like land.
   - **Richness.** Every cell that already holds an underground deposit has its richness multiplied by `lerp(1 - s/2, 1 + s/2, d / farthest)`, where `s` is the scaling, `d` the cell's distance and `farthest` the largest distance on the map (at least 1). The result is clamped to `[TerrainSubsurfaceStage.MinimumRichness, 1]`, that is 0.05 to 1. A cell with no deposit is skipped, so the pass never places one.
   - Because this runs before the kit, the deposits the kit stamps keep their richness of 0.5.
2. **Growth.** At `StartAreaRadius` 0 the stage stops here: no areas, no kit and no neutral sites. Otherwise one queue is seeded with every start's headquarters footprint (`GridFootprint.Cells(origin, kit.HqFootprint)`) in start order, and every area grows from it at once over 4-neighbours. A contested cell goes to whichever area reached it first. A cell is claimed when it is unclaimed, dry, not `TerrainRelief.Mountains`, not `TerrainKindCatalog.Standard.BlockedByDefault`, within `radius²` of its own origin, and no other start's area holds a cell within `kit.AreaGap` (Chebyshev) of it. A footprint cell that is already claimed or too close to another area is skipped and the start gets the problem `footprint_overlap`. Every claimed cell is 4-connected to its own footprint by construction. The result is written to `world.CellStartArea` (0 none, k+1 start k).
3. **Exits and size.** Exits are area cells outside the footprint that are 4-adjacent to one of its sides (corners do not count). Fewer than `kit.ExitCount` gives `no_exit`. An area with fewer cells than `kit.MinAreaCells` (or, when that is 0, `ceil(0.6 × π × radius²)`) gives `area_too_small`.
4. **Kit, per start and per `PerPlayer` entry, in authored order.** Neutral entries are skipped here; step 6 places them. The entry's resource is looked up with `resources.Find`. Not found gives `unknown_resource:<id>`; a Liquid resource gives `kit_entry_unplaceable:<id>`. Candidates are the area's cells outside the footprint whose terrain and relief the resource `Supports`, ordered by `TerrainGeometry.Hash01` of the cell, the seed and the entry index.
   - **Surface** entries write `world.Resource`. Relaxation levels, tried in order until `Count` is placed: 0 as authored (inside the `[MinDistance, MaxDistance]` band, on an empty cell, no same resource within 4 cells); 1 the band widened to the whole area; 2 the same-resource spacing dropped; 3 an existing Bonus-category resource that is not itself a kit placement may be overwritten. Supported ground, dryness and relief are never relaxed.
   - **Underground** entries are satisfied, with no placement recorded, if any area cell already holds that underground id. Otherwise each placement stamps a radius-2 deposit (richness 0.5, depth from the definition) on empty underground cells of this start's area whose terrain and relief the resource `Supports` - the rule the subsurface stage lays every deposit by - at level 0 inside the band, then level 1 anywhere in the area.
   - Still short: `missing_critical:<id>` for a critical entry, `missing:<id>` otherwise. Each placement records its relaxation level.
5. **Reports.** Appends one `TerrainStartAreaReport` per start to `world.StartAreas`, then pushes one `GD.PushWarning` listing every unusable start with its problems.
6. **Neutral sites, after every start's kit (FEAT-14).** A contested site takes what the players' own areas left, never the reverse. Each `Neutral` entry, in authored order, aims for `Count × starts` placements.
   - With fewer than two starts the entry reports `neutral_needs_two_starts:<id>` and places nothing: one start has no "between". Otherwise its resource is looked up as for the kit (`unknown_resource:<id>`, `kit_entry_unplaceable:<id>`).
   - **The band** is computed at most once per build. A tile is in it when all of these hold: it is in no area; no area holds a cell within `kit.AreaGap` (Chebyshev) of it; it is claimable (dry, not `TerrainRelief.Mountains`, not blocked by default); and its nearest start is at most `NeutralBandCells` (2) cells nearer than its second-nearest. Both distances are Euclidean, to the start cells.
   - Candidates are band tiles whose terrain and relief the resource `Supports`, in the same seeded hash order as the kit. Placement goes through the same core and relaxation order as a start's kit. `MinDistance` and `MaxDistance` are measured to the nearest start, and `MaxDistance` 0 means no upper bound. Relaxation level 1 widens that to the whole band; the band itself is never relaxed. An underground placement stamps its radius-2 deposit on empty underground cells that belong to no area (owner 0) and whose ground the resource `Supports`.
   - Still short: `missing_critical:<id>` for a critical entry, `missing:<id>` otherwise.
   - When the kit has at least one Neutral entry, the stage writes `world.NeutralSites` (a `TerrainNeutralSitesReport`) and, if it holds problems, pushes one `GD.PushWarning` listing them. A neutral problem never makes a start unusable.

## Dependencies

- Reads `TerrainGenerationSettings.StartDistanceScaling`, `.StartAreaRadius` and `.Seed`.
- Reads `TerrainGenerationBuffer.StartPositions`, `CellsWide`, `CellsHigh`, `CellIndex`, `CellInBounds`, `CellWater`, `CellRelief`, `CellTerrain`, `IntScratchA` (the growth queue), `FloatScratchA` (the squared start distances) and `CellUndergroundResource`.
- Writes `TerrainGenerationBuffer.CellStartDistance`, `CellStartArea`, `Resource`, `CellUndergroundResource`, `CellUndergroundRichness`, `CellUndergroundDepth`, `StartAreas` and `NeutralSites`.
- Reads `TerrainStartKitRules` (footprint, exits, gap, minimum size, entries and each entry's `Scope`), `TerrainResourceRules.Find` and `TerrainResourceRules.Entry` (`Id`, `Stratum`, `Category`, `Depth`, `Supports`), `TerrainEuclideanDistance.Squared`, `TerrainSubsurfaceStage.MinimumRichness`, `GridFootprint.Cells`, `TerrainGeometry.Neighbours4` and `Hash01`, and `TerrainKindCatalog.Standard.BlockedByDefault`.
- Called by `TerrainFieldBuilder.BuildPrepared` after `TerrainStartPositionStage`. Its output reaches consumers through `GeneratedTerrainField.StartAreaAtCell` and `StartAreas`: `TerrainGeneratorComponent.StartAreaAt`/`GetStartAreaReports`, the generated-cell handoff (`terrain_start_area` on `GridCellDataComponent`), `TerrainDataLayersComponent.StartAreaAt` and `TerrainMapOverlayComponent.ShowStartAreas`. The start distance reaches them through `GeneratedTerrainField.StartDistanceAtCell` (`TerrainGeneratorComponent.StartDistanceAt`, `TerrainDataLayersComponent.StartDistanceAt`); it is not part of the handoff. The neutral sites reach them through `GeneratedTerrainField.NeutralSites` (`TerrainGeneratorComponent.GetNeutralSiteReport`) and the `neutral_placements` diagnostic.

## Notes

- The Bonus test for relaxation level 3 reads the captured `TerrainResourceRules.Entry.Category` of the map's own catalog, not `TerrainResourceStage.CategoryOf`, which searches every shipped catalog and could answer for an id this map's catalog does not hold.
- `TerrainSubsurfaceStage.MinimumRichness` (0.05) is the thinnest deposit the subsurface stage lays. The start-distance pass keeps a scaled deposit at or above it, so a cell never says it holds a resource with nothing to take. The upper clamp, 1, is the top of the range the richness bands are read against, so the far country saturates rather than leaving that range.
- A neutral underground deposit is held to the band only at its centre, the recorded placement. The rest of its radius-2 disc may reach empty cells outside the areas beyond the band, the gap ring included, but only where the resource's own catalog entry `Supports` the terrain and relief, as for a start's own deposit. Before that rule, rims ran under ground the catalog says never holds the resource. On the probe's crowded map that was 32 cells, and the rule is what the `unsupported` count guards.
- Growth and the kit's distance bands use integer cell arithmetic. The start distance comes from the exact transform, rounded, and the neutral band compares Euclidean distances to the start cells. Area cells are listed in scan order and candidates are ordered by a seeded hash with the cell index as tie-break, so a seed and a kit produce the same areas, distances and placements on every run.
- `tests/TerrainGenerationBaselineSmoke.cs` hashes the `start_area` and `start_reports` layers; its `SnapshotWithStartAreas` (radius 8, default kit plus two critical wheat at 2–6 cells and one stone at 3–8) backs the two `*_start_areas` baseline cases. Without start areas both layers hash an all-zero area and no reports, and every earlier layer of the existing cases is unchanged. FEAT-14 adds the `start_distance` and `neutral_sites` layers and `SnapshotWithStartDistance`: the same radius and kit plus a Neutral horses and a Neutral iron entry (Count 1 each) at scaling 1, recorded as the two `*_start_distance` cases. With the scaling at 0 every cell hashes -1, and without a Neutral entry the site layer is the hash of an empty report, so every earlier layer of the eight older cases is unchanged.
- `tests/terrain_start_area_probe.gd` checks, on a small Continents map with seed 31415 and radius 10, that every area cell is dry, not mountainous and 4-connected to its start, that no two areas are 8-adjacent, that each headquarters footprint is level and inside its area, that report counts match the map, that usable starts meet the exit count and hold a critical wheat entry placed on a one-cell band, that relaxation was used, and that the diagnostics agree with the reports.
- `tests/terrain_start_distance_probe.gd` (registered in `run_terrain_integration.ps1`) covers FEAT-14 against the map, not only the reports.
  - **Far country.** A 112×80 Continents world, seed 31415, with two starts and the Oil And Gas set at abundant resources, read at scaling 0 and again at scaling 1. At 0 every cell reads -1 and the neutral report is empty. At 1 every start reads 0 and every cell matches a brute-force rounded Euclidean distance. Every deposit keeps its kind and depth and carries exactly the clamped factor, and the surface resources do not move. The ratio of mean richness within 10 cells of a start to the mean beyond 30 must fall against the same map unscaled. The plain "near mean below far mean" test could not fail here, because this map's deposits are already richer far from its starts.
  - **Crowded starts.** A 64×48 Continents map with six starts, radius-10 areas and Neutral horses, iron and an unknown id (Count 2 each). Every neutral placement is in the band, outside every area and its gap, dry, not mountainous, not lava, and on the map. No resource is placed more than `Count × starts` times, any shortfall is reported, the unknown id is reported, and `neutral_placements` matches the report.
  - **One start.** The same map at one start reports `neutral_needs_two_starts:horses`.
  - **Kit invariants** (through `tests/TerrainStartKitSmoke.cs`). An entry with scope 7 fails capture, and the error names the entry and the value. On the crowded map with a per-player iron entry and a Neutral iron entry, both scopes stamp deposits and no underground cell anywhere lies under ground its resource does not support. **Mutations:** scope validation removed (the entry is accepted silently); the rim's `Supports` test removed (32 unsupported cells).
  - Each section returns true from its last line, and `run` checks it. A runtime error aborts a GDScript function without recording a failure. When the smoke class was first missing from `Beep.Godot.csproj` (tests are excluded by default and each smoke is listed), the probe still printed OK until this check was added.
